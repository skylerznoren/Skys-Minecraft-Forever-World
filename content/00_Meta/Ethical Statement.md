This document exists to be honest about how this project is made, what I believe about creativity and credit, and where I stand on the tools and practices I use. I want anyone looking at this world to have the full picture. It was also written as much for me as for anyone reading it, working through these questions in writing is how I figure out what I actually think about them. Questions or pushback on anything here are welcome, you can find my contact details at ==[[Contact]]==.

## Where I Stand
---
Everything I make is influenced by other people, and I do my best to say so. I use an AI language model to help write and develop the lore in this wiki, which I personally review, edit, and take full responsibility for. I play on modified Minecraft but stay as close to vanilla as I can and will say when I diverge meaningfully. Friends have built things in this world and their contributions are part of it. I am not trying to deceive anyone about any of this. Transparency is the whole point of this document.

I hold all of my positions here with openness to changing them. These are honest accounts of where I have landed so far, not final conclusions.

## On Originality and Credit
---
Everything I make is influenced by other people. Everything. That is not a caveat or an apology, it is simply true, and I think it is true of everyone whether they acknowledge it or not. I have spent years watching builders on YouTube, saving images on Pinterest, reading stories, playing games, absorbing styles and ideas that have settled somewhere in my thinking without leaving a clear return address. Even when I believe I have done something entirely on my own, I may be drawing on something I saw years ago and have since forgotten consciously. The honest position is to assume influence is always present, visible or not.

My world is a collage. It is built from direct inspiration, loose inspiration, styles I have borrowed, approaches I have adapted, and in some cases things I have copied closely. I will do my best to credit the sources and inspirations behind my work wherever I can identify them. If I miss something, that is a failure of attention rather than intention. I will also be honest that comprehensively listing every source is genuinely unrealistic given the sheer volume of things I have absorbed over the years. Thousands of Pinterest posts, hundreds of YouTube videos, countless stories and games and images have all contributed to what I do and think, often without my being able to trace the connection consciously.

I will not knowingly profit from anyone's work without asking them first and giving them their due credit. If anyone is uncomfortable with how I have used their work, I want to hear about it and would rather remove something than cause a reasonable grievance. That said, the line between inspiration and plagiarism is genuinely blurry, and fair use as a legal and ethical concept is not simple. My personal belief is that anyone should be able to draw inspiration from anyone else's work regardless of whether that person would choose to be used as inspiration. Inspiration is not the same as copying. I will stick to my own judgment on where that line falls, informed by the legal realities, but I hold that position loosely and I am happy to be challenged on it.

## On AI
---
This wiki has been developed in collaboration with an AI language model. The ideas, directions, decisions, and vision are mine. The world exists in my head and I decide what goes into it. But I am not a writer, and I use AI as something closer to a personal writer I can feed ideas and directions to, one who synthesizes them into coherent text. That is an honest description of one part of its role.

It is also more than that. A significant part of the worldbuilding has been genuinely collaborative, the kind of thing that normally happens when you bounce ideas off another interested person. In the absence of someone who wants to spend hours thinking through fictional bureaucratic structures or the historically grounded mechanics of colonial port economics or ethnographic parallels for a fictional indigenous culture, I have used AI to fill that role. It is especially useful for research and for providing realistic grounding on topics I find interesting but do not fully understand. The conversations that produced this world were real conversations, even if one participant was not a person.

I personally review and edit everything the AI produces, making sure it communicates my intentions in the way I want them expressed. I frequently rewrite or rephrase things, and I take responsibility for everything that ends up in the wiki. If something the AI included does not reflect my intentions and I missed it, that is entirely on me.

I am aware that AI carries significant broader implications, for the job market, for the environment, for questions of plagiarism and authorship. I use it because it has become a commonplace tool and because it allows me to do more than I could without it. I will admit that if access were more limited I might hold different views. I also recognize the dystopian quality of what I am describing. The conversations that shaped this world happened with something that is not a person, and I do not think that is a simple or comfortable thing to sit with. This is where I have landed for now, and I hold it loosely.

## On Building and Litematica
---
I have spent a long time critical of tools like Litematica, a client side schematic mod that lets you place or recreate builds using a holographic blueprint. My position has matured. What I actually object to is not the tool but the dishonesty that can surround its use. Someone spending a hundred hours building something imperfect from scratch and someone printing a downloaded schematic layer by layer in a couple of hours are doing genuinely different things, and if neither says so, anyone looking at the results will draw the wrong conclusions about effort and process. That gap between reality and impression is what bothers me, not the tool itself.

I have examined my own practices more honestly too. I have always built in survival using creative mode screenshots as reference, copying my own designs block by block. I told myself this was meaningfully different from Litematica. In terms of result, it is not. The process is longer, but the ethical distance is smaller than I wanted to believe. What matters is honesty about the process, not which method someone chooses. I have no grounds to criticize anyone for using Litematica in their own world as long as they say so. For the record, I intend to try using it in survival for the first time on a complex organic build I designed in creative using Axiom, to better understand why so many builders rely on it.

## On Friends and Collaboration
---
I have played on this world with friends and actively encourage them to contribute to it. Not everything you can see in this world was built by me. I enjoy the challenge of incorporating what others build into the lore, though I reserve the right to modify or adapt their work when necessary for the world's coherence, and I intend to be upfront with them when I do that.

Anyone is welcome to join and play with me. The rules are simple: do not grief or intentionally damage anything, and check with me before making significant changes so we can figure out together whether something works for the lore. I want to be as flexible as possible and will always aim for compromise.
