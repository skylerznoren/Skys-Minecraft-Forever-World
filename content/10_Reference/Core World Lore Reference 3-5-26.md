# Minecraft Forever World — Core Reference

## What This Is

A long-term Minecraft survival world built to feel like a believable, historically layered, living setting. The goal is not a collection of builds — it is a single interconnected world with geography, culture, economics, and history that inform every decision about what gets built, where, and why.

The world has no name given to it by an outside observer. Different peoples call it different things in their own languages. That naming is intentional and ongoing.

---

## The Creator's Role

The player-creator exists entirely outside the lore. The world is being built as it exists at a specific moment in time, but the creator is not a character within it. Inhabitants have no awareness of this. Unexplained phenomena — floating terrain, ruined portals, strange generation — are cosmological facts of the world that its peoples may mythologize but cannot understand. This is tracked in the Meta folder of the wiki.

The creator also maintains a separate personal notes folder outside the wiki for things like coordinates, spawner locations, and project planning — outside the lore entirely.

---

## Core Design Philosophy

- **Environment shapes civilization.** Biome and geography determine culture, architecture, and industry.
- **Everything is connected.** Regions link through roads, rivers, and trade. Specialization creates interdependence.
- **Minecraft mechanics are lore sources.** Real game systems come first. In-world explanations follow from them, not the other way around.
- **Moldy lore.** The world feels old, layered, and partially forgotten. Traditions, ruins, and folklore hint at a deeper past that inhabitants only partly understand. Not everything is explained. Fragments, conflicting beliefs, and half-remembered histories are features, not gaps.
- **Grounded and restrained.** No overproduction. No purple prose. Leave room to breathe.
- **RDR2 and Tolkien are the tonal north stars.** Every corner of the world should reward curiosity. Things exist for reasons. The world feels lived in.

The world is currently medieval in character, but the long-term vision includes expanding into other themes, time periods, and build styles as the world grows — handled carefully so it remains coherent.

## Geography

The current active area of the world covers roughly 4000x4000 blocks, centered on a large inland sea. The world extends far beyond this — exploration has reached tens of thousands of blocks out — but the core of the lore and building is within this region. There is also a smaller southern sea south of the central landmass, between the trading town area and the jungle temple/stronghold region.

### Flower Forest Peninsula
World spawn. A peninsula of flower forest on the northeast side of the central sea. A plains biome lies across a river to the west on a separate landmass. Home to the Flower Forest Village and House Ambrose. The dominant culture is Apiarianism.

### Cleared Forest / Ruined Portal
North of the flower forest, past a thin isthmus. A large section of forest was cleared, leaving only large oaks. A ruined portal sits in the center. Future plans undecided.

### The Split Village
On a strategic isthmus northeast of the flower forest, between the flower forest region and the Great Swamp. An artificial river now bisects it. No developed lore yet, but positioned as a natural cultural and trade waypoint between the two regions.

### The Great Swamp
Far northeast. The most lore-dense region alongside the trading town. Contains the Thornspire Heartwood, the village of Reedmarsh, and the Cauldron Tap. Home to the Swampfolk. Seat of the Verdant Veins. Shaped by the history of the Witch's Sundering.

### Guano Grotto
A small rocky island in the central sea. A neutral pirate haven built around harvesting guano for gunpowder. A standing truce makes it open to all pirates regardless of affiliation. Houses a creeper farm within the terrain — in-world, this is the guano operation.

### The Trading Town & Bay *(unnamed)*
South-center of the map. The economic heart of the world. The terrain is a plains biome at roughly y=90 in the south, sloping northeast down a valley toward a natural bay. The valley has a sheer cliff on the north side that reaches nearly 80 blocks above sea level further east. Features:

- A native village predating colonization, whose people have revered the surrounding mountains for as long as memory holds. A hereditary cartographer class lives here — their purpose is mapping, and the rest of the village organizes around supporting them across generations.
- Colonial settlers arrived centuries ago. Initial contact was peaceful and friendly. Over generations the town grew, absorbing native land and culture. Many natives intermarried with settlers, and many abandoned their traditions. This loss is ongoing and mirrors real-world colonial history — it is not a clean or heroic story.
- The town now has: extensive docks in the bay, a main commercial street, tiered housing, a smelting and ship repair facility on the water, cranes moving cargo to a trade yard, an auto-sorting system (in-world: trade yard with inspection, storage, and crafting), and bulk storage excavated into the eastern mountain.
- A lighthouse stands on a stony peninsula forming the bay. The hermit who operates it is an old man — a native descendant whose father married a settler woman, was rejected by the native community, built the lighthouse after his wife died in a shipwreck, and passed it to his son. The hermit is that son. His story connects the town's colonial history to the native people's loss.
- Ancient secrets are buried under the town. Details TBD.
- A rival faction of uncertain nature lives in the mountains to the east. The town has an uneasy coexistence with them. They are misunderstood rather than simply hostile.

**West of the bay:** Stony cliffs blend into plains near a small river gully and hilly area north of the mountain. Hill-dwelling people may live here. A river mouth opens into the northern sea. The river runs south toward a stony isthmus planned to be carved into a guarded causeway connecting to the southern sea, with a bridge over the gully connecting both sides.

### Plains-Jungle Border Village *(unnamed)*
Southwest, near the trading town. On the edge of plains and sparse jungle. Plans to develop jungle-connected lore and link it to the trading town.

### The Jungle
Southwest. A large circular lake surrounded by high jungle mountains — a naturally enclosed geography. Suggests an insular, self-contained lost civilization. Plans for temples and ruins. Not yet developed.

### Southern Island Village *(unnamed)*
A small island in the southern sea with a natural village on its north side. Two smaller islands nearby. Potential noted, no developed lore yet.

### Jungle Temple & Stronghold
Far southwest. A jungle temple at the jungle's edge near a river, savannah to the west. The nearest stronghold to the world center lies directly below. A shaft has been excavated. High potential for lore development around the temple, the deep hole, and the visible end portal below.

## Peoples & Factions

**Swampfolk** — The cultural group native to the Great Swamp. Deeply tied to the swamp spiritually and practically. They regard the Thornspire Heartwood as sacred and act as its guardians. They trade certain goods outward but are being exploited by outside forces.

**The Swampfolk of Reedmarsh** — The specific community of the village of Reedmarsh. The eldest among them remember the installation of the Cauldron Tap.

**Exiled Witches** — Once kin to the Swampfolk, practitioners of herbal craft and subtle magic rooted in balance. Some delved into potion-making and vein-binding that bent the Verdant Veins to their will rather than working in harmony with them. Their hunger for control fractured the community, resulting in exile during the Witch's Sundering. Now scattered — some operate in secrecy, others in open defiance. Their craft involves potion-brewing, vein-binding, root-carving, and enchantments that redirect growth and flow.

**Morwenna of the Hollow** — Once a respected healer and seer of Reedmarsh, attuned to the Verdant Veins. Her obsession with amplifying her magic led her to advocate for deeper Mireblood extraction and accelerated ritual. She became the central architect of the schism that produced the witches and the driving force behind the Witch's Sundering. Her current dwelling, Morwenna's Abode, is rumored to lie deep in an untouched region of swamp beyond the Heartwood's reach, where the Verdant Veins run thick and unregulated. Its location is known to almost no one.

**Unnamed Exploiters of the Swamp** — The outside faction operating the Cauldron Tap. They extract and sell Mireblood on the black market. Their identity is not yet established.

**House Ambrose** — The wealthiest and most prominent family in Flower Forest Village. Publicly the exemplars of Apiarian devotion. Secretly operate a nether-based apiary that keeps bees working without rest — exploiting the absence of a day cycle in the Nether to produce honey continuously. Only the parents and eldest child know. The honey has a subtly different flavor, interpreted by outsiders as proof of divine favor. This secret is a profound theological corruption: within Apiarianism, what they are doing is monstrous.

**The Cartographers** — A hereditary class within the native village near the trading town. Their purpose is mapping. The whole village has organized itself around supporting them across generations. What exactly they map and why has not been fully established.

**Mountain Faction** *(unnamed)* — Lives in the mountains east of the trading town. Misunderstood. Uneasy coexistence with the town. Not yet developed.

**Pirates of Guano Grotto** — A loose coalition bound by the neutral island truce. Not yet developed beyond the basic premise.

**Brackfolk** — Associated with the Great Swamp. Live in the brackish river and swamp water. Not yet developed.

## History

**The Witch's Sundering** — The formal rupture between the Swampfolk and the witches. Precipitated by Morwenna of the Hollow and others who pushed for deeper magical extraction and control over the Verdant Veins. The split was nonviolent in its final form but left deep cultural and magical wounds. Its effects still shape the swamp region today.

**The Pact of Root & Reed** — A formal agreement between Reedmarsh and the Thornspire Heartwood community. Signed by Fensar Reedhand (10th great-grandfather of the current elder of Reedmarsh) and Taarvin Mossbough (a revered leader of the Heartwood and the pact's principal drafter). Likely addressed shared stewardship of waterways, resource access, and mutual defense. Details under review.

**The Arrival of the Cauldron Tap** — Within living memory of the oldest elders of Reedmarsh. An outside faction installed a device that mechanically extracts Mireblood from the swamp, disrupting the Verdant Veins. Still ongoing.

**The Colonization of the Trading Town** — Centuries ago, settlers arrived at the bay, made peaceful contact with the native village, and gradually built the town. Over generations the town consumed native land and culture. Intermarriage, cultural abandonment, and quiet displacement characterize this history. It is ongoing in effect even now.

## Key Concepts

**Apiarianism** — The system of beliefs, customs, and ritual practices centered on bees in the Flower Forest villages. What began as folk tradition has developed into a near-religious framework shaping social hierarchy, moral expectations, and interpretations of fortune. Bees are regarded as intermediaries between humanity and the natural world — living symbols of order, devotion, and sacred labor. Their discipline and communal loyalty are moral exemplars. Core tenets: deliberately harming a bee is taboo. Honey has healing and spiritual properties. The irony of House Ambrose's secret operation against these values is central to the tension of the flower forest region.

**The Verdant Veins** — Natural magical energy currents running beneath the Great Swamp and the Thornspire Heartwood. They connect water, soil, roots, and living things into a single living network — the circulatory system of the land.

**Mireblood** — A physical substance formed within the Verdant Veins system. Centuries of decaying plants, animals, minerals, and magical residue concentrating in the deep peat produces a thick alchemical liquid — condensed magic. In-world explanation for witch farm drops (redstone, glowstone, gunpowder, spider eyes, etc.).

**The Thornspire Heartwood** — An island of old-growth spruce within the Great Swamp. The ancient tree at its center is the anchor point of the Verdant Veins, stabilizing and strengthening the magical currents through its roots. Sacred to the Swampfolk, who act as its guardians.

**The Cauldron Tap** — A mechanical extraction device installed by outside exploiters, built over a witch hut. Harvests Mireblood in large quantities, draining and destabilizing the Verdant Veins. In-world explanation for the witch farm.

**The Nether Apiary** — House Ambrose's secret. Bees in the Nether have no day cycle and produce honey without rest. In-world this is slavery within the Apiarian framework — bees as forced laborers in a place antithetical to their sacred nature.

**Moldy Lore** — The governing worldbuilding style. The world feels old, layered, and partially decayed with history. Traditions, ruins, and folklore hint at deeper pasts that inhabitants only partly understand. Conflicting accounts, half-forgotten events, and unexplained remnants are intentional. Not everything gets resolved.

## Collaboration Rules

- Follow the creator's lead. Do not name things, invent characters, or expand lore without being asked.
- Minecraft mechanics are always potential lore sources — look for the in-world explanation first.
- Suggest, don't inject. Ideas are welcome. Uninvited elaboration is not.
- Plain prose, clean headers, no callout boxes, no purple prose.
- Moldy lore means leaving gaps on purpose. Resist the urge to explain everything.
