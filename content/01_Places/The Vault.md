The Vault is an ancient structure of unknown origin buried in the rock beneath [[Main Trading Town|the trading town]], one face of it forming the southern wall of a natural cavern near bedrock. It predates the town entirely, and by all evidence predates any civilization currently known to the world. It was discovered during the excavation of the kelp farm beneath [[Brownigan & Sons|the factory]] and has been in the exclusive knowledge and custody of [[The Brownigan Organization|the Brownigan Organization]] ever since. No account of who built it, when, or why has survived in any form that anyone has found or understood. Its effects on the mind and on the world are far from fully known.

## The Cavern
---
The natural cavern that houses the Vault's exposed face is enormous, roughly fifty blocks tall and largely enclosed. The only natural means of entry is through an inconspicuous cave at the northern end of the cavern, descending steeply from the cave system above. The cave opens into the cavern from behind, offering no immediate view of the Vault to the south. What the space contains only becomes apparent as one moves through it.

Rising from the cavern floor near the cave entrance is a broad natural shelf of rock, its surface angled upward like a long diagonal slope toward a high point overlooking the rest of the space. It is only from the top of this vantage, walking up the slope as the Vault gradually reveals itself to the south, that the full geometry of the structure becomes visible at once. The organization has since built the control room into the wall at roughly the same elevation, but the rock was there long before they arrived.

A large fan installed by the organization once circulated air through the cavern as a final precautionary measure against gas accumulation. It has since broken down. ==The effect this has had on the cavern environment and on those who work in it is still being established, though the organization's behavior in recent years has not gone unremarked upon by those paying close attention.==

Along one wall of the cavern, cordoned off by the organization with caution markers and barriers, sits a small collection of artifacts left behind by a people who were here long before the factory was built above. ==The nature of these artifacts and their connection to the native village's oral history are still being established.== The organization recognized their significance immediately and has not disturbed them, though understanding what they mean has proven beyond anyone currently working in the facility.

## The Vault
---
The southern wall of the cavern is the Vault itself, its face presenting a geometry too precise to be natural and too alien to be the work of any builder the town's inhabitants would recognize. Viewed from the top of the rock slope, or from the control room built at the same elevation, its shape resolves into nested squares offset at forty-five degrees, a form that has a way of staying with those who have seen it properly, surfacing unbidden in idle moments long after they have left the cavern behind.

At its heart is the Shulker Nexus, the central rotated square of the symbol, visible through the Vault's outer structure as a dark prismatic mass of black and deep violet threaded through with pulsing veins of iridescent purple that catch and hold the eye. It glows faintly and irregularly, as though breathing. An arm extends from the back wall of the Vault to the Nexus, a later addition installed by the organization to access and extract what it produces. The Nexus has begun growing onto the arm in ways that were not anticipated, having spread very slowly since the arm's installation outward along its surface in tendrils of the same dark crystalline material that comprises it.

A tube runs from the Nexus through the arm and out through the base of the Vault's lower section, installed by the organization to transport the shells produced inside out into the cavern. ==Where exactly the shells are routed from there, and how they make their way up to the surface for distribution, is still being established.==

## The Control Room
---
Set into the cavern wall at roughly the same elevation as the top of the natural rock slope, the control room houses the primary lever that activates the Nexus and an observation window looking out across the cavern toward the Vault's face to the south. It, along with the top of the rock, is the only position in the entire space from which the Vault's geometry resolves fully. ==The full contents and layout of the control room are still being established.==

## The Ventilation Shaft
---
Directly above the top of the Vault, the organization constructed a ventilation shaft roughly thirty blocks across and sixty blocks tall, an engineering undertaking whose full scale is only apparent from within. Its original purpose was straightforward: to draw the byproduct gas the Vault produces upward and away before it could accumulate in the cavern below to dangerous levels. What the organization did not anticipate was that the gas, once drawn into the shaft, would begin condensing along its inner walls into dense crystalline formations, dark and faintly luminescent, that have to be periodically cleared to keep the fans functioning. The upper facility was subsequently expanded to study this process. The shaft is large enough to be entered and traversed by a person, a practical consideration for the workers tasked with clearing it.

With the cavern fan broken and no immediate replacement installed, trace amounts of gas have begun permeating the lower cavern. ==The consequences of this are still being established.==

## The Construction
---
Building the infrastructure around the Vault, the ventilation shaft, the control room, the arm, the tube, and the upper facility above, took the better part of five decades and cost more than [[Avon Brownigan]] ever formally accounted for. It also cost considerably more than he knew.

Workers for the project were brought in from overseas, deliberately sourced from distant places where a man might disappear from his former life without raising questions that would find their way back to the trading town. Approximately a hundred and fifty men passed through the operation across those decades. Avon had given standing orders that workers showing signs of psychological deterioration were to be rotated out and replaced, a precaution he considered reasonable and sufficient. What he did not know, or did not choose to know, was the extent of what deterioration meant in practice. The men who managed the project on his behalf handled those situations themselves, and what they reported upward was a carefully edited version of what was actually happening below.

The Vault has a way of staying in the mind of those who spend time near it. For most workers that meant restlessness and vivid dreams. For some it meant something worse, a progressive unraveling that the rotation schedule did not reliably prevent. How many men were genuinely destroyed by the work, as opposed to merely marked by it, is not recorded in any document that has been found. The organization's archives contain certain gaps that those who assembled them were careful to leave unaddressed.

## The Discovery
---
The excavation of the kelp farm beneath the factory ran late shifts, and on the night of the discovery the last person remaining was a mid-level worker, one step above the general laborers, whose job it was to stay until the day's work was properly finished before locking up. Working alone, he discovered a small gap that had broken through into a cave where there should have been solid rock. He had enough seniority to know he should report it and enough curiosity to look first.

The cave descended steeply, becoming nearly vertical in places, and opened at last into the cavern, at the foot of a large rock slope. The Vault revealed itself gradually as he climbed, assembling out of the dark ahead of him, and by the time he reached the top and saw it fully the Nexus was already working on him. The deep violet veins pulsed through its surface in long slow rhythms, the whole structure seeming to breathe, and he stood at the top of that rock and could not look away and could not afterward have said how many hours long he stood there.

He found his way out eventually, covering up the entrance to the cave and establishing a new area of excavation on the other side of the hole. The compulsion to say nothing about what he had seen was strong at first, and he obeyed it for several days, during which the symbol began appearing in his hands when he wasn't paying attention, scratched into surfaces before he was fully aware he had done it. When he finally came forward it was not through choice so much as through the collapse of his ability to hold himself together. He went to the head foreman of the project. The foreman went to [[Avon Brownigan]].

Avon went to see it himself despite the worker's desperate pleading, the man by that point reduced to something barely recognizable as the person who had broken through that wall only days before. Avon looked at the Vault from the top of the rock and did not succumb, or told himself he did not, taking in and processing the full scene in only a matter of minutes. He sealed the site, had every account suppressed, and began building what would become the organization around it. The symbol burned into Avon's mind from that vantage became the emblem of everything he built for the rest of his life, a fact he never explained to anyone.

The worker was paid generously, told to move far from the trading town, and informed that his family would be provided for. He moved. His wife took their child and left him soon thereafter, driven out by the scribblings that covered every surface of the house and the ramblings that had replaced ordinary conversation. He took his own life sometime after that. The compensation continued to be paid into an account in his name for years before anyone in the organization noticed there was no one left to collect it.

Avon was relatively early in his tenure as head of [[Brownigan & Sons]] when he first saw the Vault. The remaining decades of his life were shaped by it in ways that those around him could see but never fully account for.
