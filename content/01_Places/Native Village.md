==The native village has no settled name yet.== It sits along the eastern face of the western mountain above [[Main Trading Town|the trading town]], its houses perched on the cliff sides and extending up the slope in the way that a community builds when it has occupied a place for long enough that the place has shaped it entirely. The village is oriented eastward, toward the open land and the sunrise, with the mountain at its back. It has been here considerably longer than the town below.

## Description
---
The houses climb the cliff face in a way that looks precarious from the harbor below and feels natural from within, each structure making use of the rock rather than imposing on it. The elevation is practical as much as cultural, the cartographers who form the heart of the community work from height, reading the landscape across a broader sightline than the flat ground would allow. ==The specific layout and architecture of the village are still being established.==

Worn pathways run around and along the mountain, cut into the rock over thousands of years of continuous use. They predate every road in the colonial town below. Some of them have been adopted by settlers as convenient routes without any awareness of what they are or how long they have been there.

## History
---
The village predates the colonial settlement by thousands of years. Its people had mapped the surrounding land, including the cave systems beneath the plains, long before the first settlers arrived. At some point in the distant past an encounter in those caves drove them permanently to the surface, an event whose literal details have been entirely absorbed into mythology but whose prohibition, never go below ground, has survived intact across every generation since.

The arrival of the settlers brought a period of mostly peaceful contact that gradually became something more damaging than conflict might have been. The land below the mountain was absorbed slowly. Community members who intermarried with settlers and moved to the town below rarely returned in any meaningful sense. The village has grown smaller over the centuries of the colonial period, though it has never disappeared, and its cartographers have never stopped working.

## Significance
---
The village sits directly above a town that was built largely on land that belonged to its people, and looks down over it from the mountain in a way that is geographic fact and cultural metaphor simultaneously. The cartographers' maps document every change to the landscape below, including the changes the settlers made. That record exists and accumulates, read by the community it belongs to and by no one else.
