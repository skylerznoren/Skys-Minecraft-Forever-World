[[The Great Swamp]] is a vast wetland region far to the northeast of the [[Flower Forest Peninsula]], spanning a far greater area than most outsiders who have encountered its edges would suspect. It is the seat of the [[Verdant Veins]] and home to several distinct communities whose lives are shaped entirely by it.

## Description
---
The swamp is open and sparse by nature, its dark water broken by stretches of soft ground and dotted with gnarled oak. It is not a place of dense canopy or easy navigation. The southeastern portion of the swamp, anchored by the [[Thornspire Heartwood]] and its surrounding communities, is the most inhabited and the most stable. The Verdant Veins run strongest here, kept in balance by the mother tree's roots spreading outward beneath the water and soil.

To the northwest the swamp grows wilder. The magical currents weaken with distance from the Heartwood, and the land reflects this. A second biome island sits in this northern reach, surrounded by dense mangrove growth, and the area around it has a reputation among swamp dwellers as a place where the old rules of the swamp do not apply as reliably. Few from the settled southeastern communities venture there without reason.

## Inhabitants
---
The swamp supports several communities with distinct identities. The [[Swampfolk]] of [[Reedmarsh]] are its most established settlers, bound to the land through generations of tradition and the [[Pact of Root & Reed]]. The druidic people of the [[Thornspire Heartwood]] rarely leave their forest but are deeply intertwined with the swamp's health. The [[Brackfolk]] inhabit the brackish waterways threading through the wetlands. The northern reaches are associated with evil, witches, and [[Morwenna of the Hollow]], whose rumored dwelling lies somewhere within the wilder mangrove country far from the Heartwood's reach.

## Threats
---
The [[Cauldron Tap]], installed within living memory of Reedmarsh's eldest inhabitants, sits to the west of the village and draws [[Mireblood]] from the swamp in quantities the land was never meant to give. Its effects on the Verdant Veins are ongoing and cumulative. The identity of those who operate it and their broader intentions remain unclear to the swamp's inhabitants.
