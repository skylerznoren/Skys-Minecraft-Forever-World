The Lighthouse stands on a stony peninsula above the northern harbor of [[Main Trading Town|the trading town]], visible from a great distance out across the sea. It was built by a native man who had left his community, lost his wife, and been turned away when he tried to return, and it has been tended by his son ever since. It is one of the most important structures in the harbor and one of the least remarked upon by the people who depend on it.

## Description
---
The lighthouse sits at the highest point of the stony peninsula, its light oriented outward across the sea and the harbor mouth. Beside it stands a small house, modest and weathered, where the hermit's father built a home for his family and where [[Hermit of the Lighthouse|the hermit]] has lived his entire life. The two structures together occupy the peninsula's clifftop, looking down over the northern docks and the tavern below. ==Further details of the lighthouse's appearance and construction are to be established.==

## History
---
The lighthouse's builder was a native man from the village on the western mountain who had, over years of contact with the settler town, grown fond of its people and eventually married a settler woman. His community rejected him for it. He made his life in the town regardless, and for a time it seemed to have worked.

His wife died in ==a notable storm whose broader history is documented elsewhere==, lost at sea in the dark. He tried to return to the native village afterward, his infant son in his arms, and was turned away. He took his son to the cliff above the harbor, built the house there, and began the longer work of the lighthouse. He finished it. He tended it until he couldn't. He passed it to his son and died.

The storm that took his wife was not the first to bring ships to grief in the harbor, but it was the one that produced the lighthouse, which has since made the harbor considerably safer. Whether the town has ever formally acknowledged this is not recorded.

## Significance
---
The lighthouse is practically indispensable to the harbor's operation, orienting ships navigating toward the bay at night or in poor conditions. Its builder's story is known to very few in the town, and the connection between his personal loss and the structure that has protected countless sailors since is not something most residents have thought to draw. The hermit who tends it now does not advertise the history. His father's logbook remains in the house beside the light, along with whatever else his father left behind.
