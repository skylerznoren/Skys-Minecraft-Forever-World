The Trade Yard is the commercial processing hub of [[Main Trading Town|the trading town]], occupying an elevated plateau to the south of the harbor where all incoming and outgoing cargo is officially documented, weighed, and taxed. It is the mechanism through which the port's economy runs on paper, and the place where the town's civic and commercial interests have historically met, overlapped, and occasionally disagreed. [[Penny Brixen|Penny "Brix" Brixen]] designed and built the original apparatus during the town's founding period. What exists today has grown considerably from what she left behind.

## Description
---
The plateau sits above the harbor to the south, its cliffside face carved back to make room for the yard's operations. Cargo arriving by ship is lifted from the southern docks by the cranes owned by [[Brownigan & Sons]], deposited at yard level, and logged by a yard worker stationed at the crane landing before it moves any further into the system. Goods leaving the yard by cart pass through the customs gate on the southern side at the base of the cliff, where permits and documentation are checked both entering and leaving. The double accounting between the crane landing and the customs gate is the yard's primary mechanism for catching discrepancies.

At the center of the plateau sits the Weighhouse, the yard's main building and the point through which all smaller cargo is officially processed and stored. Its central interior floor is open, with chests lining the interior edges for storage of diverse goods, and a large opening in the ceiling above that makes airborne entry possible. Along the eastern exterior, an overhang covers a loading area ==where goods can be weighed and certified without being fully unloaded, and where the weighmaster records every transaction. The certificate issued here is the legally binding basis for the goods passing through==. Smaller offices along the northern exterior handle the paperwork surrounding that central function, contracts, merchant negotiations, and the various administrative work that attaches itself naturally to any place where money changes hands regularly.

A narrow passage along the northwestern edge of the plateau toward the main street, out of the way of the yard's main traffic, houses smaller booths and offices where merchant agents conduct negotiations on behalf of traders who are not present in person. At the foot of the cliff along the southern edge of the yard, beside the customs gate, a small depression in the cliffside contains a cart repair area where a blacksmith and carpenter work to keep the yard's transport infrastructure functional.

To the east of the repair area, a natural cave opens into the cliffside and serves as the town's bulk storage, its cool and dry interior well suited to goods that need to be held for any length of time. ==The deeper reaches of the cave system, including the possibility of a salt mine or preserved goods storage further in, are still being established.== A path from within the cave curves upward and emerges at the top of the cliff above the yard, connecting the trade yard level to the upper part of the town.

Beneath the Weighhouse, a network of underground rooms houses much of the machinery that runs the sorting and storage system above, with passages that connect toward the factory. ==The nature and full extent of these underground connections are still being established.==
## History
---
The trade yard was established by [[Penny Brixen|Penny "Brix" Brixen]] in the town's founding period as a practical solution to the problems a growing harbor settlement inevitably produces. Brix designed the customs apparatus, the documentation process, and the physical layout of the plateau with the straightforward intention of making trade orderly and accountable. She left once the work was done. [[Josiah Juxton]] installed a loyalist in her place almost immediately after her departure, absorbing the trade yard's administrative function into his sphere of civic influence and beginning the process by which trade documentation and town governance became difficult to fully separate.

The yard has expanded significantly since its founding, accumulating infrastructure, underground space, and institutional complexity across the two centuries of the town's growth. The Weighhouse as it stands today is considerably larger than Brix's original building, though the core logic of the system she designed remains intact beneath the additions.

## Function
---
The trade yard handles the processing and documentation of commercial cargo moving through the port. It does not handle the taxation of the town's shops and businesses, which runs separately through ==a tax office connected to the town hall, details to be established==. Its revenue comes from fees charged on every transaction processed through the yard, every cargo logged, every certificate issued, every contract witnessed. That fee structure makes the yard's income directly proportional to the volume of trade passing through the port, a fact that has shaped its relationship to [[Brownigan & Sons]] across the company's entire history.
