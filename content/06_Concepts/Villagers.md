## Appearance

Villagers are humanoid folk with broad faces, pronounced noses, and expressive, wide-set eyes. Their skin tones range from sun-browned tan to pale clay, reflecting regional climates.

Their voices carry a distinctive nasal timbre, and they often gesture with deliberate, slow movements when speaking. Clothing is practical and suited to trade or environment, frequently adorned with small charms, woven patterns, or symbols of their home settlement.

***
## Origins & History

The earliest Villagers are believed to have been communal agrarians, bound together by shared survival and a talent for trade. Over generations, they spread across plains, forests, mountains, swamps, and coasts, adapting to local conditions.

In some regions, they developed distinct subcultures, such as mountainfolk or swampfolk, while retaining a shared language and recognizable customs.

***
## Culture

Villager society is built around mutual reliance and trade. While generally peaceful, they are protective of their homes and wary of disruption.

Each settlement typically selects or inherits a leader, such as an elder, chief, or head merchant, who mediates disputes and organizes communal labor. Rituals tied to planting, harvest, and seasonal change are common across most Villager communities.

***
## Relations with Others

Villagers are curious but cautious toward outsiders. Strong trade networks form when trust is established, but communities may quickly close ranks if threatened.

They respect the skills of other peoples yet often view themselves as the steady hands of civilization, maintaining continuity while others pursue ambition or conflict.

***
## Special Traits

Villagers possess a natural aptitude for bartering, resource management, and craftsmanship. They are resilient in harsh conditions and capable of rebuilding after disaster.

Despite their unassuming appearance, many demonstrate practical cleverness and adaptability.
