The Ambrose Secret is the hidden practice maintained by [[House Ambrose]] beneath their estate in the [[Flower Forest Village]], and the true source of the family's extraordinary honey yields, their distinctive product, and the foundation of their wealth and prominence.

## Description
---
Beneath the Ambrose estate, a hidden passage leads to a concealed nether portal. On the other side, the family maintains a fully operational apiary within the Nether itself. Because the Nether has no day cycle, bees kept there produce honey without pause, never entering their natural resting state. The operation runs continuously, generating yields that no overworld apiary could ever match.

The conditions of the Nether subtly alter the honey produced there. It returns from the portal with a deeper amber color and a richness of flavor that sets it apart from anything produced in the overworld. This subtle quality, combined with the sheer volume of output, is what has built the Ambrose reputation across the region.

## Function
---
The operation supplies the lion's share of what House Ambrose sells as their own honey, kept deliberately separate from the village collective stock at the [[Ambrose Honey Exchange]]. The difference in color and taste is presented to buyers as the natural result of exceptional stewardship and divine favor. No buyer has ever been given cause to doubt this.

## Those Who Know
---
Knowledge of the secret is passed from parent to chosen heir in a single private exchange, binding them to silence. At present, [[Melisande Ambrose|Melisande]], [[Edric Ambrose|Edric]], and [[Lucien Ambrose]] are the only three people aware of its existence. The rest of the household, including [[Anthea Ambrose|Anthea]], [[Isolde Ambrose|Isolde]], and [[Rowan Ambrose|Rowan]] have no knowledge of it.

Within [[Apiarianism]], what the Nether Apiary represents is straightforward: bees kept in a place of perpetual darkness and heat, stripped of their natural rhythms and forced into unending labor. By the values the Ambrose family publicly upholds, it is an unforgivable practice.
