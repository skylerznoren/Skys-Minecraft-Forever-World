The Cartographers are a hereditary class within the [[Native Village|native village]] whose practice of mapping the land over time is the community's central cultural function. They do not map for navigation or for practical administrative purposes. They map because the land changes, and because those changes are understood as meaningful communications from the living earth, and because someone must pay attention carefully enough and consistently enough across generations to read what is being said.

## Practice
---
The cartographers work from elevation, the cliff face of the western mountain giving them the broad sightlines the work requires. They document changes to the terrain across the surrounding area over time, comparing their maps against those of previous generations to identify patterns, shifts, and anomalies. The practice is both empirical and devotional, rigorous in its observation and framed entirely within a belief system that understands the land as animate and communicative.

Their maps are oriented with east at the top when hung on walls, reflecting the community's primary cardinal orientation toward the sunrise and the open land. Maps laid flat follow the real world orientation. The maps themselves function as multiple things simultaneously: records of geological change, communal memory, genealogy of the land, and accumulated translation of what the earth has been saying across centuries of observation.

The rest of the village organizes itself around supporting this work across generations, ensuring the cartographers have what they need to continue uninterrupted. The role passes within families but is understood as belonging to the community rather than to any individual.

## The Caves
---
The cartographers once mapped the cave systems beneath the plains surrounding the town, understanding them as thresholds into the interior of the living earth and therefore significant to any complete account of the landscape. That practice ended after an encounter in the caves so long ago that no written account exists. What remains is oral mythology describing a presence beneath the earth that their ancestors approached and fled from, and a prohibition against going below ground that has never been broken in any account the community holds.

The settlers covering cave entrances across the plains is documented in the cartographers' maps as a change to the landscape, recorded without editorial comment in the way all changes are recorded. The maps now carry, among everything else, a generation by generation account of those closures.

## Archives
---
The cartographers maintain an archive of maps stretching back further than any other record in the region. ==The physical location of this archive and its current state are still being established.== What it contains, read carefully across its full span, is a record of how the land around the trading town has changed across thousands of years of human presence, colonial and pre-colonial alike. Whether anyone outside the community has ever seen it is not recorded.
