The [[Swampfolk]] are the human peoples native to [[The Great Swamp|the Great Swamp]] and its surrounding wetlands, most notably centered around the community of [[Reedmarsh]] and the broader region of the southeastern swamp. They are defined less by a formal identity than by a shared relationship with the land they inhabit and the [[Verdant Veins]] that run beneath it.

## Description
---
Swampfolk dress and move in ways shaped by their environment; tall-booted, reed-cloaked, and at ease in terrain that discourages most outsiders. Their settlements are built with the landscape rather than against it, using materials the swamp provides and positioning structures to work with water rather than redirect it. The deep greens and purples common in their dress are traditional enough that few among them would think to explain the colors, though outsiders familiar with the swamp sometimes note their resemblance to the [[Mireblood|living currants]] of the land itself.

## Values
---
Balance, restraint, and continuity form the foundation of Swampfolk identity. The swamp gives what it gives, and the expectation is that its people behave accordingly. Extraction without acknowledgment is considered both dangerous and disrespectful. Magic is practiced sparingly and with intention, a tradition that carries the long shadow of the [[Witch's Sundering]] even among those who could not say precisely why restraint matters so much to them.

Outsiders are received with guarded politeness. Hospitality is extended so long as no harm comes to the land or its people. Those who violate that are not welcomed twice.

## Role in the Region
---
The Swampfolk understand themselves as stewards of the swamp rather than its rulers. They monitor the health of the Verdant Veins, maintain the old rites, and keep watch over those who would exploit [[Mireblood]] for profit or power. The arrival of the [[Cauldron Tap]] and the presence of the [[Sap Suckers]] in the region represents the most direct challenge to that role in living memory.
