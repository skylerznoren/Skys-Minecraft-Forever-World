Brownigan & Sons is the trading town's dominant industrial operation, a factory and associated enterprise occupying the southwestern corner of the harbor. Founded in the earliest period of colonial settlement and expanded across three generations of [[The Brownigan Family|the Brownigan family]], it has shaped the town's economy and infrastructure more than any other private institution. Its public face is a legitimate and well-regarded industrial concern. What lies beneath it is known to very few.

## Description
---
The factory sits at the southwestern corner of the harbor, with cranes on their own foundations nearby moving cargo between docked ships and the trade yard above. The cranes are owned outright by the factory and are the sole means of moving heavy commercial cargo efficiently from the water to yard level, a piece of infrastructure the port depends on entirely. The ground floor is open and accessible, with a wide entryway for carts and workers to move through freely. A single large industrial furnace occupies the back of the factory, its firebox feeding upward through a naturally tapering stone smokestack that narrows as it rises before finishing in a more modern stack at the top. The furnace handles all of the factory's smelting work, from ore to glass to brick. Along the eastern side of the southern wall, a bank of smokers runs continuously beside the crane pallet, drying kelp harvested from the farm below and compressing it into fuel sold to supply the industrial furnace and to sell to ships resupplying at the harbor. An upper floor looks down over the factory floor, where ==a private office that once belonged to Avon Brownigan still contains his desk, his portrait hanging above the chair, and paintings of his father and grandfather on the wall behind it. The current state of this room is to be established.==

Beneath the factory, excavated during Avon Brownigan's expansion of the operation, lies a large kelp farm feeding the smokers above. During that excavation a worker broke through into a cave that led to something that would change the character of Brownigan & Sons forever.

## History
---
[[Winfield Brownigan]] arrived with the first wave of colonial settlers and established a modest workshop and refinery at the harbor's edge, one of several small operations serving the needs of a growing settlement. His son [[Manfred Brownigan|Manfred]] expanded it substantially, constructing the factory building and establishing it as a serious industrial concern. It was [[Avon Brownigan]] who transformed it into what it is today.

Avon inherited the company at roughly twenty-five and ran it for approximately seventy years. He excavated the kelp farm beneath the factory floor, built the cranes, and established quiet supply arrangements with businesses throughout the town, delivering processed materials directly and making Brownigan & Sons structurally indispensable to the local economy. He also offered a premium sealed packaging service to wealthy merchants, shipping their most valuable goods inside shulker boxes at a price no competitor could match and a reliability of preservation none could replicate. The cranes he built were never formally transferred to public ownership, giving him a degree of leverage over the harbor's throughput that no civic authority ever successfully challenged.

When a worker excavating the kelp farm broke through into a cave that led down to a vast cavern near bedrock, Avon covered it up immediately. What he found there and what he built in response is documented in the articles on [[The Vault|the vault]] and [[The Brownigan Organization|the Brownigan Organization]].

Avon died approximately sixty years ago without a clear heir and without passing the full picture of his operation to anyone. The company passed to his most trusted senior administrator, who continued running it competently and without full knowledge of what lay beneath. It remained in the hands of company management for the decades that followed, until [[Bramwell Trembly|Bram Brownigan]] arrived in the trading town by chance, took work at the factory, and was recognized through his papers as Avon's grandson through Avon's daughter. After some internal division, he assumed ownership. He has held the position for a few years now.

## Function
---
Brownigan & Sons processes raw materials on behalf of merchants, miners, and traders passing through the port, taking a cut of output as its processing fee. It smelts ore, casts metal fittings and ship parts for the harbor's repair trade, fires sand into glass and clay into brick, and dries kelp into fuel sold directly to ships at the dock. It supplies many of the town's businesses with processed materials through standing arrangements, bypassing the trade yard entirely. The cranes handle all heavy cargo movement between the southern docks and the trade yard above.

## Structure
---
The company is privately owned, currently by Bram Brownigan. Day to day operations are managed by senior staff whose families have held their roles across multiple generations. Below them the factory employs a permanent workforce of laborers, furnace operators, and crane handlers, with additional seasonal workers during periods of high trade. Bram is well liked on the floor and known for treating workers generously.

A separate and compartmentalized operation exists within the company that Bram has no knowledge of. Its structure and activities are documented under [[The Brownigan Organization|the Brownigan Organization]].
