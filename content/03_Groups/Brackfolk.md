The Brackfolk are an amphibious people inhabiting the submerged ravines, root caverns, and brackish waterways of [[The Great Swamp|the Great Swamp]]. They are almost entirely unknown to the surface communities of the region, including [[Reedmarsh]], whose residents share the same waterways without any awareness of what moves beneath them. Among themselves they are known as the **Glaudren**, meaning roughly people of the grey-green murk, a name that reflects both their appearance and the waters they inhabit.

## Origins
---
The Glaudren are a splinter of the [[Thalvaen]], the ocean-dwelling peoples of the open sea to the south. Generations ago, Thalvaen groups followed seasonal fish runs and freshwater trade routes inland through the river mouths and brackish estuaries south of the swamp. Over centuries of adaptation to the murkier, shallower inland waters, they changed. Their skin darkened and mottled for camouflage in murky water. Their hands and feet webbed. Their eyes grew smaller and more sensitive, suited to navigating dim underwater spaces rather than open ocean. What emerged was something distinct from their ocean kin, shaped entirely by the environment they had chosen.

Whether the Glaudren still maintain any awareness of their Thalvaen origins, or whether that history has become mythologized beyond recognition after so many generations underwater, is not something the surface world has any way of knowing.

## Description
---
The Glaudren are physically adapted to a dual existence. In water their mottled grey-green coloring, webbed digits, and slightly reflective eyes mark them clearly as something other than surface dwellers. After extended time out of water their features shift toward something more ordinary, their scales dulling and their appearance blending well enough with surface villagers to pass unnoticed in poor light or at a distance. Most retreat to water nightly regardless, as prolonged dryness weakens them. Surface folklore around the swamp occasionally references river wives and waterchangers, figures who arrive with the mist and vanish after rain. These stories are closer to the truth than the people telling them realize.

## Moorholdt
---
The primary known settlement of the Glaudren in the southeastern swamp is Moorholdt, the dark grove, a submerged hall built among the vast root systems of a ravine south of the [[Cauldron Tap]]. It is constructed within and around the root networks rather than against them, its chambers lit by bioluminescent growth that the Glaudren have cultivated over generations. It serves as a spiritual and diplomatic center for the clans of the region. No surface dweller has ever been brought inside under normal circumstances.

## The Drowned Sisters
---
Ten generations ago, during the [[Witch's Sundering]] and its aftermath, several of those exiled from [[Reedmarsh]] fled into the deeper swamp waterways using water-breathing preparations. They eventually found Moorholdt and were, after a period of cautious observation, accepted by the Glaudren clans. These figures, remembered among the Glaudren as the Drowned Sisters, brought surface alchemical knowledge with them that blended over time with Glaudren traditions. The Glaudren had no inherited knowledge of the witches' history with [[Mireblood]] overuse and carried no suspicion toward them as a result.

## Relationship to the Surface World
---
The Glaudren maintain their secrecy as a foundational principle. Surface communities are kept ignorant of their settlements and their nature. Individual Glaudren occasionally move among surface dwellers when necessary, passing well enough in dim conditions or at a distance to avoid scrutiny. These interactions are practical rather than social.

The arrival of the [[Cauldron Tap]] disrupted water flow and drew Mireblood-rich sediment away from root systems and submerged groves that the Glaudren consider sacred. Their response has been quiet and persistent, dismantling unattended machinery, tangling equipment, and scattering cargo when the opportunity presents itself. In this they are acting in parallel with the younger [[Swampfolk of Reedmarsh|Swampfolk of Reedmarsh]], though neither group is aware the other is doing the same thing.
