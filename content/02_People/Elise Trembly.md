Elise Trembly, born Elise Brownigan, was the only child of [[Avon Brownigan]] and the mother of [[Bramwell Trembly|Bram Brownigan]]. She left the trading town at sixteen and never returned, taking nothing of her father's name or legacy with her. She raised her son alone in a woodland cottage far from any town of note and died of a sudden illness when Bram was twenty-one. She is not widely remembered in the trading town. Most people there never knew she existed.

## Background
---
Elise was born when Avon was around seventy, the only child he ever fathered. Her mother named her and left Avon not long after, his bitterness at having a daughter rather than a son having made the marriage an increasingly cold thing. Avon had wanted an heir in his own image, and Elise was not that. His subsequent attempts to father another child came to nothing, and he turned his attention back entirely to the factory and the legacy he was building there, never coming to understand that she was the legacy he had been looking for all along. She was given everything money could provide and almost nothing else, kept at the edges of the Brownigan name, known to few, permitted little, and loved by her father in no way she could feel. Any friendships she formed in the harbor town dissolved as people moved on, as they tend to do in places where ships are always leaving.

At sixteen she had enough. She packed what little she cared about, slipped past her caretaker, and stowed away on a departing ship without knowing or particularly minding where it was headed. She took her diary with her but never wrote in it again after that day.

## Life After
---
The years that followed were not something Elise spoke of in any detail. She made only general mention of her travels to Bram, the places she passed through, the things she learned, and the beliefs she came to hold, the kind of wisdom that accumulates slowly and doesn't attach easily to specific memories. The specifics she kept to herself.

At nineteen she found someone. She spoke of his father openly and with warmth, his beliefs, his way of seeing the world, the future he had been working toward, but of how their time together ended she said nothing, and Bram learned not to ask. She took his name, Trembly, and named her son after him. She settled in a woodland cottage far from the roads and harbors and particular cruelties of the world she had grown up in, and raised Bram in that place with patience and deliberate warmth, the kind of mother she had needed and never had. She called him Bray.

The values she passed to him were her most lasting work. She died of a sudden illness when he was twenty-one, taken in a matter of weeks, and he did not recover quickly. It was only after her death that he found her diary, hidden in a chest beneath her bed. What it contained is known only to him.

## Relationships
---
Elise's relationship to Avon was one of neglect on his part and quiet severance on hers. She did not speak of him to Bram, and whether she thought of him in her later years is not recorded anywhere she chose to leave behind. Her relationship to her son was the defining thing about her, and the only version of herself she seems to have wanted to pass on.
