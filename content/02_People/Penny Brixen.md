Penny Brixen, who went by Brix, was the woman who designed and built the customs and trade apparatus that underpins [[Main Trading Town|the trading town's]] economy. She arrived with the first wave of colonial settlers, alone while most others came with families, established the [[The Trade Yard|trade yard]] and its systems from the ground up, and left once the work was done, returning to wherever she had come from without ceremony or record. She had no interest in what the institution became after her. Most of what became of it was not what she would have chosen.

## Background
---
Little is known of Brix's origins, background, and reasons for being among the founding settlers, She arrived with practical knowledge and a clear sense of purpose, neither of which she appears to have explained to anyone in terms that survived in any account. She came alone, which was unusual enough among the founding company to be noted, and left the same way.

## Role
---
Brix designed the customs gate, the weighing and certification process, the documentation system, and the physical layout of the trade yard plateau with the straightforward intention of making commerce orderly and accountable. The core logic of what she built has remained intact across two centuries of additions and expansions, which is the most reliable measure of how well she did the work.

She left shortly after the system was functioning. [[Josiah Juxton]] installed a loyalist in her place almost immediately after she left, absorbing the trade yard's administrative function into his own sphere of influence. Whether Brix was aware this would happen, and whether she cared, is not recorded.

## Legacy
---
Brix is remembered in the trading town as a founding figure in the practical rather than the civic sense, the person who made the port work rather than the person who governed it. Her name appears in early trade records and in the institutional memory of the yard itself, where the system she designed is simply called the system, with no particular attribution attached to it anymore.
