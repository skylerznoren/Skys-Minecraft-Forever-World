Bramwell Trembly, known professionally as Bram Brownigan, is the current head of [[Brownigan & Sons]]. He is the grandson of [[Avon Brownigan]] through Avon's only daughter [[Elise Trembly]], and came to the position not through inheritance or intention but through a combination of chance, circumstance, and a set of papers proving who he was. He has held the role for a few years. He is the kind of person who is kind rather than just, generous rather than scrutinizing, and well liked for it. Whether those qualities will serve him in the role he has inherited is another question.

## Background
---
Bram was raised by his mother Elise in a cottage in the woods, far from the trading town and from any knowledge of his grandfather's name or legacy. Elise (who called him Bray) gave him an unhurried childhood and instilled in him a genuine concern for the people around him that has stayed with him into adulthood. When he was twenty-one she died of a sudden illness, taken in a matter of weeks. He was inconsolable for months, unable to leave the home they had shared. Eventually he did, if not entirely for his own sake then for hers.

The years that followed were nomadic. He worked odd jobs across a number of ports and settlements, accumulating life experience without any particular direction. He arrived at [[Main Trading Town|the trading town]] by chance and found work at the factory without knowing whose factory it was. It was the senior staff who eventually recognized the connection, cross-referencing his papers against old company records. The process that followed was not smooth. There were those within the company who had managed things comfortably for decades and were not eager to see a Brownigan return. Clear heads eventually prevailed, and Bram assumed ownership.

## Role
---
Bram runs the factory with genuine enthusiasm and no apparent awareness of its deeper history. He walks the floor most mornings, remembers names, and has a reputation for paying workers fairly and resolving disputes quickly. His instinct in most situations is generosity, which his staff have learned to rely on and which occasionally costs him more than it should.

His weakness is not cruelty or indifference but naivety. He leads with his heart more readily than his head and tends to assume the best of situations that warrant closer scrutiny. He has not asked the questions about his inheritance that a more suspicious man would have asked on the first day. It has not occurred to him that there are questions to ask. Those who resent his presence or find his good nature unconvincing occasionally call him Ram, a pointed shortening of his given name that implies a certain blundering quality he would be the last to recognize in himself.

## Relationships
---
Bram has no meaningful relationship with [[The Brownigan Organization|the Brownigan Organization]] because he does not know it exists. The organization is aware of him and uncertain what his presence means for their continued operation. The senior staff who oversee the factory's legitimate functions occupy an uncomfortable middle ground, aware of more than Bram but less than they believe.

His connection to Avon is entirely mediated by the portrait on the office wall and the stories that older workers tell, which are not always flattering and which Bram listens to more carefully than people expect.
