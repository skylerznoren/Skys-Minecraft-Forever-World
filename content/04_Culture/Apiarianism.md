[[Apiarianism]] is the system of beliefs, customs, and ritual practices centered on bees in the [[Flower Forest Village|Flower Forest villages]]. What began as a founding philosophy has shaped every aspect of village culture, from social expectations to interpretations of fortune and misfortune.

## Origins
---
Apiarianism has no single founder that history remembers by name. The earliest settlers of the flower forest are said to have observed the bees around them — their discipline, communal labor, and quiet interdependence — and built their community around those same principles. The philosophy that emerged was simple: live as the bees live. That idea proved sturdy enough to outlast the people who first held it.

## Beliefs
---
Bees are regarded as moral exemplars rather than mere insects. Their labor, loyalty to the colony, and defense of the hive are virtues the community holds itself to. Core tenets include that deliberately harming a bee is taboo, that honey carries healing properties, and that a thriving hive is a sign of a household living rightly.

## Culture
---
Prosperity is measured in hive health as much as harvest yield. Families with flourishing colonies are well regarded; those whose hives fail may be quietly pitied or, in less charitable corners of the village, suspected of some moral neglect. This has allowed certain wealthy families to frame their abundance as divine favor — a use of Apiarian symbolism that sits uneasily alongside the philosophy's humble origins.

At the turning of each harvest season, villagers share honey communally and return small offerings of comb and wax to the hives — acknowledgment that what was taken came at the bees' generosity.

Honeycomb is worked into the architecture of village buildings as both a practical material and a mark of devotion. A home with honeycomb set into its walls is understood as one that has earned its place in harmony with the colony — and one that has enough to spare.
