# Minecraft Forever World — Core Reference

## What This Is

A long-term Minecraft survival world built to feel like a believable, historically layered, living setting. The goal is not a collection of builds — it is a single interconnected world with geography, culture, economics, and history that inform every decision about what gets built, where, and why.

The world has no name given to it by an outside observer. Different peoples call it different things in their own languages. That naming is intentional and ongoing.

---

## The Creator's Role

The player-creator exists entirely outside the lore. The world is being built as it exists at a specific moment in time, but the creator is not a character within it. Inhabitants have no awareness of this. Unexplained phenomena — floating terrain, ruined portals, strange generation — are cosmological facts of the world that its peoples may mythologize but cannot understand. This is tracked in the Meta folder of the wiki.

The creator also maintains a separate personal notes folder outside the wiki for things like coordinates, spawner locations, and project planning — outside the lore entirely.

---

## Core Design Philosophy

- **Environment shapes civilization.** Biome and geography determine culture, architecture, and industry.
- **Everything is connected.** Regions link through roads, rivers, and trade. Specialization creates interdependence.
- **Minecraft mechanics are lore sources.** Real game systems come first. In-world explanations follow from them, not the other way around.
- **Moldy lore.** The world feels old, layered, and partially forgotten. Traditions, ruins, and folklore hint at a deeper past that inhabitants only partly understand. Not everything is explained. Fragments, conflicting beliefs, and half-remembered histories are features, not gaps.
- **Grounded and restrained.** No overproduction. No purple prose. Leave room to breathe.
- **RDR2 and Tolkien are the tonal north stars.** Every corner of the world should reward curiosity. Things exist for reasons. The world feels lived in.

The world is currently medieval in character, but the long-term vision includes expanding into other themes, time periods, and build styles as the world grows — handled carefully so it remains coherent.

## Geography

The current active area of the world covers roughly 4000x4000 blocks, centered on a large inland sea. The world extends far beyond this — exploration has reached tens of thousands of blocks out — but the core of the lore and building is within this region. There is also a smaller southern sea south of the central landmass, between the trading town area and the jungle temple/stronghold region.

### Flower Forest Peninsula
World spawn. A peninsula of flower forest on the northeast side of the central sea. A plains biome lies across a river to the west on a separate landmass. Home to the Flower Forest Village and House Ambrose. The dominant culture is Apiarianism.

### Cleared Forest / Ruined Portal
North of the flower forest, past a thin isthmus. A large section of forest was cleared, leaving only large oaks. A ruined portal sits in the center. Future plans undecided.

### The Split Village
On a strategic isthmus northeast of the flower forest, between the flower forest region and the Great Swamp. An artificial river now bisects it. No developed lore yet, but positioned as a natural cultural and trade waypoint between the two regions.

### The Great Swamp
Far northeast. The most lore-dense region alongside the trading town. Contains the Thornspire Heartwood, the village of Reedmarsh, and the Cauldron Tap. Home to the Swampfolk. Seat of the Verdant Veins. Shaped by the history of the Witch's Sundering.

### Guano Grotto
A small rocky island in the central sea. A neutral pirate haven built around harvesting guano for gunpowder. A standing truce makes it open to all pirates regardless of affiliation. Houses a creeper farm within the terrain — in-world, this is the guano operation.

### The Trading Town & Bay *(unnamed)*
South-center of the map. The economic heart of the world. The terrain is a plains biome at roughly y=90 in the south, sloping northeast down a valley toward a natural bay. The valley has a sheer cliff on the north side that reaches nearly 80 blocks above sea level further east. Features:

- A native village predating colonization, whose people have revered the surrounding mountains for as long as memory holds. A hereditary cartographer class lives here — their purpose is mapping, and the rest of the village organizes around supporting them across generations.
- Colonial settlers arrived centuries ago. Initial contact was peaceful and friendly. Over generations the town grew, absorbing native land and culture. Many natives intermarried with settlers, and many abandoned their traditions. This loss is ongoing and mirrors real-world colonial history — it is not a clean or heroic story.
- The town now has: extensive docks in the bay, a main commercial street, tiered housing, a smelting and ship repair facility on the water, cranes moving cargo to a trade yard, an auto-sorting system (in-world: trade yard with inspection, storage, and crafting), and bulk storage excavated into the eastern mountain.
- A lighthouse stands on a stony peninsula forming the bay. The hermit who operates it is an old man — a native descendant whose father married a settler woman, was rejected by the native community, built the lighthouse after his wife died in a shipwreck, and passed it to his son. The hermit is that son. His story connects the town's colonial history to the native people's loss.
- Ancient secrets are buried under the town. Details TBD.
- A rival faction of uncertain nature lives in the mountains to the east. The town has an uneasy coexistence with them. They are misunderstood rather than simply hostile.

**West of the bay:** Stony cliffs blend into plains near a small river gully and hilly area north of the mountain. Hill-dwelling people may live here. A river mouth opens into the northern sea. The river runs south toward a stony isthmus planned to be carved into a guarded causeway connecting to the southern sea, with a bridge over the gully connecting both sides.

### Plains-Jungle Border Village *(unnamed)*
Southwest, near the trading town. On the edge of plains and sparse jungle. Plans to develop jungle-connected lore and link it to the trading town.

### The Jungle
Southwest. A large circular lake surrounded by high jungle mountains — a naturally enclosed geography. Suggests an insular, self-contained lost civilization. Plans for temples and ruins. Not yet developed.

### Southern Island Village *(unnamed)*
A small island in the southern sea with a natural village on its north side. Two smaller islands nearby. Potential noted, no developed lore yet.

### Jungle Temple & Stronghold
Far southwest. A jungle temple at the jungle's edge near a river, savannah to the west. The nearest stronghold to the world center lies directly below. A shaft has been excavated. High potential for lore development around the temple, the deep hole, and the visible end portal below.

## Peoples & Factions

**Swampfolk** — The cultural group native to the Great Swamp. Deeply tied to the swamp spiritually and practically. Traditional dress runs to deep greens and purples, colors long associated with swamp life whose connection to the Verdant Veins and Mireblood is noted by outsiders but not consciously articulated by the Swampfolk themselves. They trade certain goods outward but are being exploited by outside forces.

**The Swampfolk of Reedmarsh** — The specific community of the village of Reedmarsh. More open to outside contact than the Thornkin but still guarded, particularly since the Cauldron Tap's installation. The eldest among them remember what the swamp felt like before it. Younger residents have begun interfering with the Tap's operations in small ways.

**The Thornkin** — The druidic people of the Thornspire Heartwood. Rarely leave the forest. Deeply protective of it and of Vethara. Their leader takes the name Mossbough upon appointment, setting aside their family name and bonding to the mother tree through that role. Historically more open to outside contact, but closed the Heartwood entirely following the Witch's Sundering when Taarvin Mossbough's grief passed into Vethara through their bond and she responded by sealing the outer approaches. The moorland at the former outer boundary is not discussed.

**Exiled Witches** — Once ordinary members of the swamp communities, not a distinct faction. Practitioners of herbal craft and subtle magic who lived alongside fishermen and farmers. The Witch's Sundering drove those who sided with Morwenna into exile. Cut off from community over generations, their descendants drifted toward the dangerous behavior the Swampfolk had feared. Some descendants of those who stayed in Reedmarsh remain in the community, practicing quietly and saying nothing about it. Witch practice is taboo even now.

**Morwenna of the Hollow** — Once a respected healer and seer of Reedmarsh, trained by the Thornkin and given access to the interior of the Heartwood. Her personal Mireblood extraction was too small to meaningfully harm the swamp, but her example drew others with less careful intentions. She sided with those followers rather than recant, and was exiled. She carried a broken branch from the root table north with her. She is alive in her abode in the northern mangrove swamp, tending a young mangrove tree grafted from that branch, the only living cutting of Vethara's lineage outside the Heartwood. She has extended her own life through alchemical means. Almost no one believes she still lives.

**Sap Suckers** — The local name for the outside faction operating the Cauldron Tap. Named after Witchsap, the outside market name for Mireblood. Their true identity and backing are unknown. The industrial character of the Tap suggests resources beyond opportunistic outside operators. They transport Mireblood in unmarked barrels by cart, presenting as ordinary traders on the road.

**House Ambrose** — The wealthiest and most prominent family in Flower Forest Village. Publicly the exemplars of Apiarian devotion. Secretly operate a nether-based apiary that keeps bees working without rest — exploiting the absence of a day cycle in the Nether to produce honey continuously. Only the parents and eldest child know. The honey has a subtly different flavor, interpreted by outsiders as proof of divine favor. This secret is a profound theological corruption: within Apiarianism, what they are doing is monstrous.

**The Cartographers** — A hereditary class within the native village near the trading town. Their purpose is mapping. The whole village has organized itself around supporting them across generations. What exactly they map and why has not been fully established.

**Mountain Faction** *(unnamed)* — Lives in the mountains east of the trading town. Misunderstood. Uneasy coexistence with the town. Not yet developed.

**Pirates of Guano Grotto** — A loose coalition bound by the neutral island truce. Not yet developed beyond the basic premise.

**Brackfolk (Glaudren)** — An amphibious people inhabiting the submerged ravines and root caverns of the Great Swamp. Known among themselves as the Glaudren, meaning people of the grey-green murk. Descended from the Thalvaen, ocean-dwelling peoples who migrated inland through the river mouths south of the swamp generations ago and adapted over centuries to brackish water. Capable of passing among surface dwellers unnoticed under certain conditions. Their primary settlement is Moorholdt, a submerged hall built among root systems in a ravine south of the Cauldron Tap. Almost entirely unknown to surface communities. Also quietly interfering with the Tap's operations.

## History

**The Witch's Sundering** — The fracture between the communities of Reedmarsh and the Thornspire Heartwood and those who became the Exiled Witches. Morwenna of the Hollow's personal Mireblood extraction drew others with less careful intentions, and the elders moved against her for what her example had caused rather than what she had done herself. The final confrontation at the root table in the Heartwood ended with Morwenna breaking a branch and walking north with her followers. Taarvin Mossbough's grief passed into Vethara through their bond, and she responded by sealing the outer Heartwood, killing back the outer extension trees and leaving the moorland. The Pact of Root & Reed was signed in the immediate aftermath to formalize the alliance between Reedmarsh and the Heartwood against the potential threat of the scattered witches.

**The Pact of Root & Reed** — A formal agreement between Reedmarsh and the Thornspire Heartwood signed by Fensar Reedhand and Taarvin Mossbough in the aftermath of the Witch's Sundering. Formalized an existing alliance in writing at the moment both communities feared violence. Addressed free passage, shared stewardship of waterways, and mutual defense obligations. Still in effect.

**The Closing of the Heartwood** — Following the Witch's Sundering, Taarvin Mossbough's grief and sense of betrayal passed into Vethara through their bond. She pulled the full strength of the Verdant Veins back toward the protected core, killing the outer extension trees and sealing every approach except the southern passage through Reedmarsh. The outer zone where traders and visitors once moved freely became the moorland. This is not discussed by the Thornkin.

**The Arrival of the Cauldron Tap** — Within living memory of the oldest elders of Reedmarsh. An outside faction built an industrial Mireblood extraction device on the site of an old witch hut west of Reedmarsh, chosen for its position at a Verdant Veins intersection. Still operating. Slowly draining the Veins and weakening Vethara, which is manifesting as physical decline in the current Mossbough.

**The Colonization of the Trading Town** — Centuries ago, settlers arrived at the bay, made peaceful contact with the native village, and gradually built the town. Over generations the town consumed native land and culture. Intermarriage, cultural abandonment, and quiet displacement characterize this history. It is ongoing in effect even now.

## Key Concepts

**Apiarianism** — The system of beliefs, customs, and ritual practices centered on bees in the Flower Forest villages. What began as folk tradition has developed into a near-religious framework shaping social hierarchy, moral expectations, and interpretations of fortune. Bees are regarded as moral exemplars — their labor, communal loyalty, and discipline are virtues humans should emulate. Core tenets: deliberately harming a bee is taboo. Honey has healing and spiritual properties. The irony of House Ambrose's secret operation against these values is central to the tension of the flower forest region.

**The Verdant Veins** — Natural magical energy currents running beneath the Great Swamp and anchored by Vethara at the Thornspire Heartwood. They connect water, soil, roots, and living things into a single living network. The Cauldron Tap is draining them. In the period before the Witch's Sundering the Veins extended further outward, brightening the surrounding swamp. After the Sundering they pulled back to their natural range. Morwenna's cultivation of Vethara's cutting in the north is beginning to produce new nascent Veins in the northern mangrove swamp.

**Mireblood (Witchsap)** — A dense, slow-moving alchemical substance forming deep beneath the swamp's peat over centuries, where organic decay and magical residue concentrate under pressure. Deep purple-black in color with a green shimmer. Refines into redstone, glowstone dust, gunpowder, spider eyes, sugar, glass bottles, and preserved sticks. Known in outside markets as Witchsap. Sacred to the Swampfolk — if not taken sparingly, it must not be taken at all. In-world explanation for witch farm drops.

**Vethara / The Mother Tree** — The sole known thornspire tree, ancient beyond record, at the center of the Thornspire Heartwood. Anchor point of the Verdant Veins. Her roots spread through the plateau and outward into the swamp. Extension trees throughout the Heartwood are physical offshoots of her root network and die without it. Her canopy drips constantly from moisture drawn up through the roots. Not conscious in the human sense but deeply responsive to stimuli, particularly those passed through the Mossbough bond. A cutting of her lineage exists in the north, tended by Morwenna. The Thornkin are unaware of this.

**The Mossbough Bond** — The connection between Vethara and the current leader of the Thornkin, who takes the name Mossbough upon appointment. Vethara receives through this bond what she otherwise cannot access — the emotional and experiential world of the person connected to her — and responds to it as a plant responds to any stimulus, completely and without deliberation. The current Mossbough is declining as the Cauldron Tap drains the Verdant Veins. The cause has not been identified.

**The Cauldron Tap** — An industrial Mireblood extraction structure built by the Sap Suckers on the site of an old witch hut west of Reedmarsh, chosen for its position at a Verdant Veins intersection. Shaped like a virus with a containment vat at the top and output channels extending outward like legs. Copper construction, now oxidized green. Residual post-processed material seeps back into the surrounding peat. In-world explanation for the witch farm.

**The Nether Apiary** — House Ambrose's secret. Bees in the Nether have no day cycle and produce honey without rest. In-world this is slavery within the Apiarian framework — bees as forced laborers in a place antithetical to their sacred nature. The honey produced has a deeper amber color and a richer flavor attributed by outsiders to divine favor.

**Moldy Lore** — The governing worldbuilding style. The world feels old, layered, and partially decayed with history. Traditions, ruins, and folklore hint at deeper pasts that inhabitants only partly understand. Conflicting accounts, half-forgotten events, and unexplained remnants are intentional. Not everything gets resolved.

## Wiki Structure (Obsidian)

Folders: Places, People, Groups, Culture, Events, Concepts, Items, Resources, Meta, and a separate 09_Notes folder outside the lore for coordinates, project planning, and personal reference. Articles use lean frontmatter (type, region, status, last_updated, tags). Connections sections are not used by default — links live in prose. Hub articles serve as entry points for major regions or topics. Tag registry and formatting rules live in the formatting reference document, which should be pasted alongside this document in new sessions.

## Collaboration Rules

- Follow the creator's lead. Do not name things, invent characters, or expand lore without being asked.
- Minecraft mechanics are always potential lore sources — look for the in-world explanation first.
- Suggest, don't inject. Ideas are welcome. Uninvited elaboration is not.
- Plain prose, clean headers, no callout boxes, no purple prose.
- Moldy lore means leaving gaps on purpose. Resist the urge to explain everything.
